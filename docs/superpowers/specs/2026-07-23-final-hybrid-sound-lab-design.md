# Final Hybrid Sound Lab Design

Date: 2026-07-23

## Goal

Build a disposable, mono-first but genuinely stereo listening lab for three
complete SHR Synth voice identities. Each identity must combine several
interacting mechanisms into a finished signal path rather than presenting an
isolated oscillator, one effect, or a starter tone. The lab must show each
identity alone, as a three-note held chord, and in a short harmonic progression.

This remains research code. No candidate enters `Engine`, presets, stable
macros, JACK/ALSA, or SHR-DAW until automated evidence and human listening both
pass.

## Interpretation of the user's guidance

The user's signal-path sketch is design guidance, not a literal mandatory
patch list. Its durable principles are:

- sound identity should emerge from interacting sources, band-specific
  nonlinear paths, resonant structures, and modulation rather than one raw
  oscillator;
- fixed-width integer VCO and register behavior is a composable quirky
  mechanism, not a requirement for an all-integer synth or a swarm of similar
  machines;
- stereo must be generated by the topology itself through asymmetric,
  cross-coupled, or moving state, not by writing a mono sample twice;
- related slow movements should use deliberately different rates so the whole
  voice does not breathe on one obvious synchronized cycle;
- a complete voice must remain intelligible in harmony, not merely impressive
  as one isolated note.

## Alternatives considered

### One universal graph with many parameter presets

This would be quick to render and easy to compare, but it would repeat the
earlier failure: parameter positions inside one graph are not fundamentally
different listening mechanisms. Rejected.

### Mono sources followed by a shared stereo-effects rack

This would make channel differences easy to produce, but stereo would be an
afterthought and the candidates could again sound like the same source under
different treatments. Rejected.

### Three complete hybrid voice topologies

Each candidate owns its pitched mechanism, machine interaction, nonlinear
routing, resonant/stereo state, and slow movement. They share only small
auditable primitives and the offline audition/measurement harness.
Selected.

## Candidate A: Cross-Coupled Machine Voice

The central source is a bounded nonlinear phase-modulated carrier with a small
prepared vibrato. One fixed-width register oscillator contributes two different
signals: a pitched state waveform perturbs modulation depth, while carry
events excite a subharmonic/resonant path. It is one mechanism inside the graph,
not a 24-member swarm.

The main source is split into low and high bands:

- the low band receives bounded overdrive whose drive/cutoff movement follows a
  slow register-derived pulse smoother;
- the high band receives a soft nonlinear gate and controlled distortion; and
- a phase-shifted subharmonic reinforces the low path without becoming a
  separate conventional oscillator bank.

Left and right then enter different short comb/resonator pairs with safe
cross-feedback. A short continuously moving micro-delay is applied
asymmetrically. Its movement rate is independent from the low-band pulse rate
and from vibrato. A centered direct component protects mono compatibility, but
must not dominate enough to hide the stereo structure.

Expected identity: a pitched mechanical voice whose internal layers seem to
push and answer one another across the stereo field.

Failure: ordinary distorted synth bass, static widening, rhythmic clicking,
unstable feedback, inaudible integer influence, or stereo that disappears
outside one short sweet spot.

## Candidate B: Spectral Shadow Voice

The central source traverses a small set of authored harmonic frames using
prepared recursive partial rotations. A single fixed-width address machine
perturbs traversal position and partial-group emphasis after smoothing. It does
not directly become the whole waveform.

A lower “shadow” path derives from divided phase/state and passes through a
bounded phase-shifting all-pass structure. The main and shadow paths feed
different left/right nonlinear filter pairs. Each channel uses a different
prepared cutoff trajectory, and a small safe cross-coupling term transfers
filter state between channels. Slow stereo motion and spectral traversal use
different non-integer-related rates.

Expected identity: one transforming spectral object with a darker delayed
shadow that sometimes aligns, sometimes separates, and changes the apparent
shape of the stereo image.

Failure: organ-like additive tone, arbitrary wavetable browsing, predictable
filter sweep, detached sub-bass, or chorus pasted onto a mono source.

## Candidate C: Dual Resonant Body

This topology does not begin with a continuously dominant oscillator. A small
tonal exciter combines a quiet nonlinear carrier, deterministic short noise
energy at note onset, and sparse fixed-width carry/borrow events. The mixture
feeds two genuinely different resonant bodies:

- one side emphasizes a pitch-locked low resonator with bounded overdrive in
  its feedback path;
- the other emphasizes dispersed higher resonances with a soft nonlinear gate;
  and
- limited cross-feedback lets energy migrate without collapsing both sides
  into the same state.

Each body also has its own short modulation delay. Their rates differ from one
another and from excitation movement. Feedback remains below a tested energy
ceiling and all coefficients are prepared outside sampling.

Expected identity: a struck/bowed hybrid object with two connected physical
bodies, audible internal motion, and a sustained pitch center.

Failure: generic Karplus-Strong pluck, static comb ringing, unstable howl,
pitchless noise, ping-pong echo, or left/right copies with different EQ.

## Shared DSP boundary

Create `dsp::hybrid` with:

- `HybridFamily::{CrossCoupledMachine, SpectralShadow, DualResonantBody}`;
- `HybridVoice::new(family, sample_rate, frequency_hz, seed)`;
- `HybridVoice::sample() -> StereoFrame`;
- `HybridVoice::reset()`; and
- introspection used only by tests/reports for declared movement rates, delay
  bounds, register widths, and topology identity.

Every voice owns fixed-size scalar state. Construction may prepare
trigonometric rotations, coefficients, note-tracked delays, and deterministic
seeds. Sampling may not allocate/deallocate, lock, perform I/O, log, format,
panic, access clocks, or perform avoidable transcendental setup.

Do not reuse the old `IntegerSwarm` as a source. Extract or independently
implement only the small fixed-width operations required by each hybrid graph,
with explicit masks and wrapping semantics.

## Harmonic audition flow

The non-real-time `hybrid` lab owns a `HybridEnsemble` containing exactly three
independent `HybridVoice` instances of the same family. Each note receives a
deterministic but different seed/initial phase so a chord is not three
sample-aligned copies. Voice mixing uses conservative fixed headroom; offline
loudness matching happens only after rendering and must not hide clipping.

Each family produces:

1. `single`: one sustained middle-register note for 10 seconds;
2. `chord`: one open three-note minor chord for 12 seconds; and
3. `progression`: four three-note chords, four seconds each, for 16 seconds.

The progression is a fixed D-minor-centered test sequence:

```text
D minor:  50, 53, 57
B-flat:   46, 50, 53
C major:  48, 52, 55
A minor:  45, 48, 52
```

This is not a theory claim or a preset commitment. It is a repeatable harmonic
stress test containing changing fundamentals, shared tones, close intervals,
and bass movement.

The lab renders nine true-stereo musical files: three conditions for each of
three families. It also renders one mono-fold progression per family as a
diagnostic listening file, for twelve WAVs total. Mono folds are evidence, not
additional musical variations.

Chord transitions use short equal-power-style gain ramps prepared and applied
without allocation in the sample loop. Each new chord constructs or resets its
three fixed voices outside the rendered transition block; the offline harness,
not the real-time DSP object, owns sequence scheduling.

## Automated evidence

Every new render path requires:

- invalid-construction rejection;
- deterministic reset and repeated-render hashes;
- finite and explicitly bounded output;
- allocation-free `HybridVoice::sample` and three-voice ensemble sampling;
- non-silence and retained pitch evidence;
- DC, peak, RMS, crest factor, and maximum adjacent-sample jump;
- declared delay and modulation-rate bounds;
- measurable but mono-compatible stereo: nonzero side energy, neither
  accidental polarity inversion nor silent fold-down, and left/right signals
  that are not sample-identical;
- three target-note projections in held chords and every progression segment;
- evidence that each family differs spectrally and temporally from the other
  two; and
- scalar workstation cost recorded only as development evidence.

Because all three graphs contain oscillation and/or nonlinearities, render an
eight-times-rate conservative residual comparison for low, middle, and high
single notes. Treat it as engineering evidence, not a perceptual score. Any
severe sample-rate dependence, especially from integer mechanisms, must be
reported rather than hidden.

Stereo measurements must use analysis windows long enough to cover at least
two cycles of the slowest declared spatial movement. The 10–16 second musical
renders satisfy this requirement. Reports must distinguish two-channel file
format from actual channel difference.

## Listening gate

The generated batch is disposable and lives under ignored `artifacts/` or a
fresh temporary directory. The README gives this order:

1. three single-note files;
2. three held chords;
3. three progressions on headphones;
4. the same progressions on speakers; and
5. the three mono-fold diagnostics.

The listener judges complete identity, harmonic usefulness, internal movement,
stereo translation, mono survival, fatigue, and whether each topology remains
meaningfully distinct. No automated metric accepts a final sound.

After the verdict, document conclusions and delete the batch unless the user
explicitly requests preservation of a specific result.
